## Overview

The design states leaves-up composition: a file's `group` pair places its own sections in the result its parent merges, a linked file is composed before its parent merges it, and a named group nests in the division of the document that placed it.

## Problem

- **Groups are flat in the design.**
  *Grouping* states a group as one top-level division, and every document whose pairs name a group joins it there, whatever links it.
- **Nothing states whose pairs place a linked file.**
  No clause says a parent's pairs leave a child's sections alone, or that an intermediate group nests inside its parent's result.

## Proposal

**The scene.** *Grouping* states membership, depth and the merged arrangement; *Dependency order* states placement order; *Presets* states that a document's presets reach its own content alone.

- **A file places its own sections.**
  A file's pair applies to its own sections in the result its parent merges; a parent's pair applies when it is bundled in turn, and the invocation bundles each entry.
- **Composition runs from the leaves up.**
  Each placed document is composed under its own pair before the document that placed it merges that result. A reference to a document already placed merges nothing more, and a link a file does not bundle stays its content.
- **A named group nests where its first member was placed.**
  It stands inside the division of the document whose reference placed its first member, after that division's own content, one level deeper per enclosing division; a group whose first member is an entry stands at the top level. A document with no pair keeps its file group at the top level.
- **The surrounding clauses read a group as a division that may nest.**
  *Groups*, *Continuous Markdown*, *Dependency order*, *Group structure*, *A document in several groups* and *Group isolation* stop stating every group as a top-level H1.
- **The pin moves.**
  The README's design link moves to the engineering commit carrying the amendment.
