**Component:** src/prosiform/cli.py

## Overview

A redirected positional entry compiles its substitute and the run derives its output filename from the substitute, and a referrer `-f` names is redirected with a fragment-only entry resolving its section against the substituted referrer. The seam that makes both true landed with W01; this row lands the evidence — two corpus fixtures and four unit cases — and corrects two predictions the epic record made about the build.

## Problem

`entry` normalizes a positional reference and `-f` through `resolve`, and `execute` derives the run's folder and bundle name from the selections those references give. W01 applied the mapping at both call sites, so a redirected entry already resolved to its substitute and `derived_name` already read the substitute's path.

What was absent was the evidence. No fixture stated that a redirected entry compiles the substitute, that the run's folder is named for the substitute rather than for the reference the caller wrote, or that a fragment-only entry against a redirected referrer resolves its section against the substitute. The epic record's `w03.md` then predicted two behaviours the build does not have: that the corpus runner always passes `-o`, and that two entries naming one substitute are refused as multiple distinct selections. Both predictions are wrong, and this session corrected the record rather than the code.

## Proposal

**The scene.** `entry(text, substitution)` normalizes a positional reference and then applies the mapping, and `execute` calls it once per positional reference and once for `-f`. `selections` reads the references the mapping returned, `run_name` builds the folder's name from them, and `derived_name` appends the bundle suffix.

**The design.**

- **The redirect reaches the entry and the referrer.** Both take the mapping at `entry`, so a fragment-only entry naming `#run` resolves against the substitute `-f` was redirected to, and a substitute carrying a fragment contributes that fragment in place of the authored one.
- **The name follows the substitute.** `derived_name` reads the reference `entry` returned, so `-m entry.md=stand-in.md entry.md` publishes `stand-in.bundles/stand-in.bundle.md`, where the same run without `-m` publishes `entry.bundles/entry.bundle.md`.
- **`-o` names the folder, not the file.** It decides where the run's folder is written and changes nothing about the name, so the derived name follows the substitute with or without it.
- **Two entries naming one substitute derive one name.** They resolve to one identity, so `selections` counts one selection and the run publishes one bundle of it. This is not the collision refusal the record predicted: that refusal is for two distinct selections deriving one name, which two keys naming one substitute do not produce.
