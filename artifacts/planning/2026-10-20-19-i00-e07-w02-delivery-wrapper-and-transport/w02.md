## Overview

The wrapper that owns the delivery contract between the build and its caller.

## Problem

A transport limit or a broken pipe can truncate a bundle that compiled correctly, and Markdown arriving is not evidence that all of it arrived.

## Proposal

The wrapper invokes the entry point as a process, gives it no standard input, and buffers both of its streams. It reads the process status first, so a run that failed is a failure however much Markdown it left on standard output. It then counts the buffered bytes against the transport's response limit, and only a count within the limit yields a document.

A bundle over the limit is a delivery failure carrying no document. The wrapper substitutes no summary, no partial document and no reference the caller must follow separately, and it keeps completion metadata, byte counts and warnings in its structured result rather than in the Markdown.

The transport names its own limit. There is no default, because the safe figure belongs to the transport rather than to the build, and a limit of zero is refused rather than read as unlimited. The limit counts encoded bytes, and no token estimate is divided out of them.

Buffering means the wrapper never breaks the entry point's pipe. A write the entry point cannot complete is already its own publication failure, which the wrapper reads as a failed run like any other.

## What This Row Does Not Carry

`AC6` asks that a successful run report each entry reference and the destination it resolved to on the diagnostic stream. The build already computes those pairs, and emitting them needs a diagnostic the code catalogue has no entry for. That entry spans two branches and changes what every successful fixture declares, which is work of its own rather than a detail of the wrapper. It is `W05`, and [D48](../2026-10-17-prosiform-v1/decisions.md) records the route.
