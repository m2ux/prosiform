## Overview

Cohort grouping: merging one part of the source tree at a named heading level, and qualifying a merged name only where it would collide.

## Problem

A composite grouped by document puts every document's `Operations` section in a different place, so a reader comparing operations across a library reads twenty groups. Grouping the whole composite by heading level instead is too blunt: the entry document and the modes are not parallel to one another and gain nothing from being merged.

Merging also breaks two things a document-grouped composite relies on. A merged document stops being contiguous, so a whole-document reference has nowhere to resolve, and content above the first merged heading belongs to no group.

A merged group draws one name from several documents. Qualifying every name with its document would impose a naming scheme on the whole composite to serve a minority of names; refusing the collision would reject sources that are otherwise composable.

## Proposal

A cohort names a directory slug and a source heading level, and is repeatable. Every document under any directory of that slug is merged at that level, beneath one heading carrying the slug. A document no cohort names keeps its own group.

A merged heading keeps its source text where that text occurs once in its group. Where two or more documents contribute one text, each takes the shortest left-anchored run of its root-relative path that distinguishes it from the others, joined to the text, so the qualifier is as short as the collision allows and appears in the emitted heading. Two headings of one document colliding in one group remains the source ambiguity the heading rules refuse, and anything still coinciding takes the renderer's ordinal.

A member document's preamble is emitted under a leading sources group as one heading per document carrying its path, which is where a whole-document reference to a member resolves. A reference to a cohort-depth heading resolves to the merged group.
