## Overview

Cohort grouping: merging one part of the source tree at a named heading level, and qualifying a merged name only where it would collide.

## Problem

A composite grouped by document puts every document's `Operations` section in a different place, so a reader comparing operations across a library reads twenty groups. Grouping the whole composite by heading level instead is too blunt: the entry document and the modes are not parallel to one another and gain nothing from being merged.

Merging also breaks two things a document-grouped composite relies on. A merged document stops being contiguous, so a whole-document reference has nowhere to resolve, and content above the first merged heading belongs to no group.

A merged group draws one name from several documents. Qualifying every name with its document would impose a naming scheme on the whole composite to serve a minority of names; refusing the collision would reject sources that are otherwise composable.

## Proposal

A cohort names a directory slug and a source heading level, and is repeatable. Every document under any directory of that slug is merged at that level, beneath one heading carrying the slug in title case. A document no cohort names keeps its own group, as does a document named as an entry reference, which is never claimed.

A merged section carrying body text from more than one document is the document's section rather than a container of named items, and branches whole: one branch heading per contributing document. A section carrying only named items does not.

A merged heading keeps its source text where that text occurs once in its group. Where two or more documents contribute one text, that text is emitted once and each document's content hangs beneath it under a branch heading naming the document: the shortest run of its root-relative path that distinguishes it from the others, in title case, measured after dropping the leading segments they all share. The document's own heading is replaced by the shared one and its content shifts one level. Two headings of one document colliding in one group remains the source ambiguity the heading rules refuse, and anything still coinciding takes the renderer's ordinal.

A cohort's heading is its directory slug in title case. A cohort carries a `Body` section holding the preamble of each member that has one, as a subsection named by the document's stem in title case less a trailing word the section already states, so `epic-issue-body.md` reads `Epic Issue`. That heading is where a whole-document reference to the member resolves. A reference to a cohort-depth heading resolves to the merged group.
