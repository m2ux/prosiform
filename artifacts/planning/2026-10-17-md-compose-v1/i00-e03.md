## Overview

Outline hierarchy makes a partial selection readable. A fragment selects one eligible section and its subsections; every emitted source heading moves down one level beneath its file heading, unless that heading already carries the document's own title; and the unselected ancestors of a selected section appear as headings alone, so the original outline survives a selection that skipped most of the document.

## Problem

A section lifted out of its document loses the structure that gave it meaning.

- **A fragment names a heading, not a region.**
  Where the named section ends is a decision, and a heading inside a list or a blockquote looks like a terminator without being one.

- **Two heading syntaxes carry one meaning.**
  A Setext heading cannot be shifted by editing a prefix, so a uniform level shift has to convert it while keeping its visible text and inline semantics.

- **A selected child without its parents reads as a top-level topic.**
  Selecting a third-level section and emitting it alone discards the relationship that placed it under its parent.

- **One document's sections are read beside another's.**
  Twenty documents in one closure carry an `Operations` section, and a composite grouped by document puts each in a different place, so comparing them means reading twenty groups.

- **A shift has a ceiling.**
  Markdown carries six heading levels, so a deep selection beneath a generated file heading can require a seventh, which has no heading spelling.

- **A fragment is renderer-defined.**
  The fragment that reaches a heading is the one the renderer generates from its visible text, including its duplicate numbering, so a lookup that invents its own spelling resolves to the wrong heading or to none.

## Proposal

- **Only document-level headings are selectable roots.**
  A section runs to the next document-level heading of equal or higher level. A heading inside a container does not terminate it, and a fragment naming one fails as an unsupported target.

- **A file group keeps its source order.**
  Selected regions and structural ancestors are emitted in the order they appear in their source, so a document's preamble sits after its filename heading and before its first retained heading, and its sections follow one another as written.

- **A cohort merges one part of the tree.**
  A cohort names a directory slug and a heading level, and every document beneath a directory of that slug is merged at that level, beneath one heading carrying the slug. A document no cohort names keeps its own group. A merged heading keeps its source text where that text occurs once; where several documents contribute one text, each takes the shortest run of its path that tells it from the others, measured after dropping the leading segments they all share.

- **Headings shift by one, and a duplicated title is absorbed.**
  Every emitted source heading in a file group, inside retained containers included, moves down exactly one level. Where the source's only H1 slugifies to the final segment of its file heading, that heading is not emitted and the group keeps its source levels instead, because the file heading already names it. Relative depth and visible text are preserved, and a Setext heading becomes the equivalent ATX heading.

- **Ancestors appear as scaffolding.**
  A selected section's unselected eligible ancestors are emitted as headings with escaped literal text and no body, siblings or other descendants. Scaffolding is recorded separately from selected content and is not traversed for dependencies.

- **Overflow fails.**
  A composition needing a heading beyond the sixth level fails rather than flattening, clamping or emitting heading-like text.

- **Source fragments come from a compatibility-tested slugger.**
  Visible heading text is derived from the event stream, and the fragment it produces is checked against fixtures covering punctuation, markup, Unicode, duplicates and collisions with naturally suffixed headings.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] **AC1.** A fragment naming an eligible heading selects that heading and the blocks up to the next document-level heading of equal or higher level, with container-nested headings not terminating it, as the extraction fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC2.** A fragment naming a container-nested heading fails with its declared error code, as the nested-target fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC3.** Each emitted source heading in a group carrying its own title sits exactly one level deeper than in its source, with its visible text and relative depth unchanged, as the shift fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC4.** A Setext heading is emitted as the equivalent ATX heading carrying the same visible text and inline semantics, as the Setext fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC5.** A selected section's unselected eligible ancestors are emitted as headings carrying no body, sibling or descendant content, as the scaffold fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC6.** A composition that would place a heading beyond the sixth level fails with its declared error code and publishes nothing, as the depth-overflow fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC7.** A source heading's fragment equals the one a GitHub renderer generates for it, as the slug compatibility fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC8.** A file group emits its selected regions and structural ancestors in their original source order, with a document's preamble before its first retained heading, as the source-order fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC9.** A source whose only H1 slugifies to the final segment of its file heading has that heading absorbed, emitting no duplicate and leaving its group's headings at their source levels, while a source whose H1 differs keeps it and shifts, as the title-absorption fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC10.** One entry composed with and without a cohort selects the same content and differs only in how it is arranged, with each cohort emitting one heading carrying its slug, a merged section emitted once however many member documents contribute to it, and a document no cohort names keeping its own group, as the grouping fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC11.** A merged heading occurring once in its group is emitted with its source text alone, while one drawn from several documents carries the shortest run of each document's path that distinguishes them after their shared leading segments are dropped, and a whole-document reference to a member resolves to that document's entry in the leading sources group, as the qualification fixtures show.
