**Component:** fixtures/limits

## Overview

The sixteen fixtures that pass a budget pass its name instead, and every expected result is unchanged.

## Problem

Sixteen manifests carry a letter, and the corpus is what proves the ceilings still bound.

- **Sixteen fixtures pass one.** `-F` in three, `-N` in four, `-L` in three, `-E` in two, and `-B`, `-I`, `-O` and `-T` in one each.
- **Each declares an expected result.** A fixture crossing a ceiling expects `E_RESOURCE_LIMIT`; one staying under it expects a bundle. Neither fact changes when the spelling does.
- **The fixtures are the evidence the collapse is faithful.** A changed expected result would mean the ceiling moved, when only the name should.
- **Two fixtures must keep their value.** `-N` bounds container nesting and YAML nesting independently, and `-L` counts authored occurrences — the rules are asymmetric, so a fixture's figure must move with its ceiling and not with another.

## Proposal

**The scene.** A manifest declares `arguments` as a list, one element per argv entry, so a budget is two elements and becomes two elements with a different word.

**The design.**

- **Each letter becomes its name.** `["-F", "3"]` becomes `["--budget", "files=3"]`, so the value travels with the name in one element.
- **No expected result changes.** The task's evidence is that every manifest's `expected` block is untouched, which states the behaviour did not move.
- **The figures stay with their ceilings.** A fixture bounding nesting keeps its nesting figure and gains the name `nesting`, so no count moves to another budget.
- **A corpus-wide check states the removal.** No manifest's `arguments` holds a retired letter, so a missed fixture fails rather than passing over an option the build no longer declares.
