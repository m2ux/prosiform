## Overview

The Structural Conformance pattern page: Liskov substitution, checked as an ML signature ascription checks a structure, realised by `subst`, and the refusal of a fragment the target does not carry, with a specimen the build compiles and checks.

## Problem

- **The grammar affords it, and nothing names it.**
  `subst`, and the refusal of a fragment the target does not carry already behave as Liskov substitution, but no page tells an author so.
- **An author re-derives it per skill.**
  Without a named pattern, each skill states its own version of the structure as private rules.

## Proposal

**The scene.** The catalogue's template and checks are in place, and the Shared Rule page shows a conforming page and specimen.

**The design.**

- **Paradigm.**
  Liskov substitution, checked as an ML signature ascription checks a structure, cited from Liskov and Wing, A Behavioral Notion of Subtyping; MacQueen, Modules for Standard ML.
- **Grammar.**
  `subst`, and the refusal of a fragment the target does not carry, each linked to its documentation section.
- **Specimen.**
  A tree at `docs/patterns/structural-conformance/`: a substitute carrying every heading its callers name by fragment.
- **What the bundle shows.**
  Each named section resolving to the substitute, committed as the specimen's expected bundle set.
- **Principles.**
  The page's `Related` names the SOLID principles it serves: Liskov substitution.
- **The limit to probe.**
  The page states whether a document can declare the headings any substitute for it must carry; a limit found is stated under `Limits` and raised as its own issue.
