## Overview

The guide `pf` prints with no arguments is where a reader learns what `⇉` asks of it. A Haiku 5.5 reader took it for usage and, where a dispatch's brief stated the dispatched work, ran that work in its own context. This hot-fix opens the guide on following a bundle and states which of a brief's statements are the dispatcher's to act on.

## Problem

- **The guide opened as usage.**
  `GUIDE` began "pf compiles a Markdown document…" and `Usage:`, and reached the symbol grammar after the exit statuses. Readers reported that `pf` printed usage only.
- **A brief's stated work read as the dispatcher's.**
  The guide stated that a brief is done before the bundle is handed on. A Contract brief's Leaves, "In the folder's `notes.md`, the word count of `source.md` under `## Count`", reads as a task under that sentence, and 4 of 8 Haiku runs of the #809 specimen did the count themselves.
- **A dispatched reader held back its own dispatches.**
  Runs that kept the work answered that they were already the dispatched agent; the guide said nothing of a sub-agent's own `⇉` lines.

## Proposal

- **The guide opens on following a bundle.**
  "Following a bundle" comes first: every instruction is followed as written, each link by the symbol grammar, and a sub-agent handed a bundle follows it the same way, handing on each `⇉` and `⫽` link it meets. The symbol grammar follows, then usage, compiling a link and exit status.
- **A brief's asks are the dispatcher's, its stated work the sub-agent's.**
  The dispatcher does what a brief asks of it before handing the bundle on; what the brief states the bundle takes is what it hands over, and what it states the bundle leaves or answers is the sub-agent's work.
