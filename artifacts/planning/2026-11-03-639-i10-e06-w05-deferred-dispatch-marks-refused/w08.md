## Overview

`↳` is the composition mark: `[↳ Title](p.md#s)` composes the section, `[¬↳ Title](p.md#s)` defers it, and an arrow label is refused, so the source grammar is `⇉`, `⫽`, `↳` and `¬`, and the agent learns only `↳` and `⇉`.

## Problem

- **Composition is spelled by an arrow nobody reads.**
  `closure.py` recognises a directive by a label of exactly one Unicode arrow, from a list of Unicode blocks, while the bundle an agent reads carries `↳` for the same material once deferred. A source author and an agent learn two marks for one placement, and a bare `→` link publishes with nothing but the arrow as its text.

## Proposal

**The scene.** `closure.py` reads operator marks and the arrow-recognition rule; `composition.py` places a directive's target beneath its scope.

- **`↳` composes.** (User, 2026-11-03.)
  A link whose text opens with `↳` and one space is an immediate composition, placed under every rule a directive has: destination sections, order, merging contributions under one title, presets and `subst`. The link published to the destination carries the link's title text.
- **`¬↳` defers.** (User, 2026-11-03.)
  `[¬↳ Title](p.md#s)` builds the same destination section and publishes the `[↳ Title](p.md#s)` line, its path rebased; the deferred-preset refusal holds.
- **Arrows are refused.** (User, 2026-11-03.)
  `[→](…)`, `[¬→](…)` and every other arrow label fail with `E_UNSUPPORTED_CONSTRUCT`, naming the link and pointing at `[↳ …]`. `[⇉↳ …]` and `[⫽↳ …]` contradict and are refused.
- **The corpus follows.**
  Every composition fixture moves to `↳`, refused-arrow fixtures are added, and the `section-composition` workflow sources of the diagram-following eval move to `↳` so its corpus test still compiles them.
