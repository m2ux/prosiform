**Component:** evals/diagram-following/workflow.py

## Overview

The compiled form's dispatch step, stated by its operators alone, and a reader that refuses the second statement a sentence beside them makes.

## Problem

The compiled form exists to measure whether an agent follows the protocol `pf` publishes. Its steps did not test that: each carried the operator *and* a sentence restating it.

- `1. Hand the review to a sub-agent: [⇉ review](review.md#check).` states the dispatch twice.
- `The diagram is the workflow. Each state's section says what to do in that state and what it hands to a sub-agent.` states it in the entry preamble of every compiled workflow.
- `1. [⇉ draft](../draft.md#choose). It continues from the question it returned.` restates the re-dispatch the `Dispatch` protocol already states.

A session could act on any of those and never read the protocol, so a compiled pass measured reading English while an authored pass measured the same, and the difference between them was not what compilation adds. The reader also required the prose: `STEP_ONE` and `STEP_SET` matched `Hand the … to a sub-agent:` and nothing else, so a compiled step became unreadable the moment its sentence was removed.

## Proposal

- **The operator is the statement, and the protocol is its meaning.**
  A compiled step is its marked links. `pf` compiles each mark to a bundle and emits the `Dispatch`, `Dispatch in parallel` or `Parallel dispatch` protocol that says what a sub-agent is handed and what a returned question does.
- **The reader identifies a compiled step by its marks.**
  `COMPILED_STEP` matches a numbered step carrying an operator, and `dispatched()` takes the targets and the togetherness from the marks rather than from prose.
- **A compiled step that repeats its protocol is a fault.**
  `restated()` names the word it repeats — `sub-agent`, `together`, or the re-dispatch continuation — and the fault quotes the line, so the author is told what their protocol already says.
- **A compiled step carrying an unmarked link is a fault.**
  A link with no operator dispatches nothing, so a step that mixes marked and unmarked links reads as a partial dispatch and is refused.
- **The authored form is untouched.**
  It has no operator and no published protocol, so its sentence is its whole statement of the dispatch and the control the comparison rests on.
