## Overview

Candidate validation stands between a constructed candidate and a published bundle. The candidate is reparsed under the declared profile and checked against the provenance recorded during construction, and nothing reaches standard output until it passes. This is the one check that must precede first use: it establishes that what the tool hands a caller is the content its sources carried.

## Problem

A bundle can be syntactically valid Markdown and still be the wrong document.

- **Surface validity proves nothing about provenance.**
  A reparse that agrees with itself establishes that the output parses, not that each region came from the source it claims, nor that a region is missing.

- **A silent loss is worse than a failure.**
  A caller handed a bundle with a region missing proceeds on it, because nothing distinguishes a short bundle from a complete one. A caller handed a failure reads its sources another way.

## Proposal

- **Candidate validation has its own policy context.**
  The candidate is reparsed under the same grammar but expects generated filename headings, shifted headings and structural ancestors. It is not passed back through frontmatter stripping, dependency discovery or generated-input rejection.

- **Invariants are checked against recorded provenance.**
  Destination integrity, exactly-once coverage, absence of new links, absence of surviving definitions, ownership stability, outline preservation and depth safety are checked against the provenance ranges and generated-structure records kept during construction, not by counting strings.

- **A failing candidate publishes nothing.**
  Standard output stays empty and the run exits nonzero, so a caller cannot mistake a refused bundling for a short bundle.

- **The remaining gate follows first use.**
  Block boundary safety, source consistency, generated-input rejection, diagnostic content and validation scope extend this check rather than condition it, and are delivered by [E08](https://github.com/m2ux/prosiform/issues/59) once the tool is in agents' hands.
