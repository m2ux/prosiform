## Overview

A merged group arranges a folder's documents under one heading. It arranges them by heading text, so a document is scattered across the sections it happens to contribute to and the group names it only where another document contributed the same text. This epic arranges the group by member instead: one section for each document the group carries, holding that document's own content, reached by one destination.

## Problem

The arrangement is keyed on heading text, so a member is neither whole nor reliably named.

- **A member is split across the sections it contributes.**
  Two documents each carrying `Style` and one heading of their own emit a shared `Style` section holding both, with the unshared headings as loose siblings. Neither document is readable as a document.

- **A member can be named nowhere.**
  A document whose only identity is its own H1 has that heading absorbed and discarded, so the group carries its content under a heading that names the heading text rather than the document.

- **Two members can share one destination.**
  A member whose content all sits at the grouping depth resolves to its first contribution, so two members whose first contribution carries one heading text resolve to one fragment: a bundle emits `[Common](#style)` beside `[Agents](#style)`.

- **A wrapper is emitted whether or not it is needed.**
  Content above the grouping depth is carried under an `Overview` section even where each member's contribution already has a heading of its own, and no grouping depth reaches the arrangement without it.

- **Generated headings carry two spellings.**
  A group heading is title-cased and a document group heading is not, so one bundle emits `# Rules` beside `# procedure` and `# rules/codex`.

## Proposal

- **A group carries one section for each member.**
  The section is titled from the member's filename stem and holds that member's content, its own headings beneath it. Nothing is keyed on heading text, so no member is split and no wrapper stands between the group and its members.

- **A member's section absorbs the heading that repeats its name.**
  Where the member's leading heading carries the member's own name, at whatever level it sits, the generated section heading is that heading, so a document titled after itself is named once.

- **A member is named by as much of its path as tells it apart.**
  Two members whose stems agree take the shortest run of their root-relative paths that distinguishes them, after the leading segments every member shares are dropped.

- **A member has one destination.**
  A whole-document reference to a member reaches that member's own section, so no two members resolve to one heading.

- **The option takes the folder alone.**
  Arranging by member leaves no source heading level to select, so the grouping rule is the folder slug and nothing else.

- **One convention covers every generated heading.**
  The title casing a group heading already takes reaches the document group headings and every other heading the compiler writes, while a heading an author wrote and a filesystem path a run names keep the spelling their source gave them.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] **AC1.** A merged group emits one section for each member it carries, titled from that member's name and holding that member's content beneath it, as the grouping fixtures over members with and without content above their first heading show.
- [ ] **AC2.** A member's generated section heading is the member's own leading heading where the two carry one name, at whatever level that heading sits, as the absorption fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC3.** Two members whose filename stems agree are named by the shortest run of their root-relative paths that distinguishes them, as the stem-clash fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC4.** A whole-document reference to a merged member resolves to that member's own section, and no two members resolve to one heading, as the destination-validation tests show.
- [ ] **AC5.** The command takes a grouping rule naming a folder alone and refuses one carrying a heading level, as the command-line tests and the help output show.
- [ ] **AC6.** Every heading the bundle generates carries one title-casing convention, nested paths and hyphenated names included, as the grouping tests and the corpus fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC7.** A heading a source authored and a filesystem path a run names carry the spelling their source wrote, as the preservation fixtures show.
- [ ] **AC8.** The documented commands and the diagrams beside them carry the headings a run of them emits, as running those commands and comparing their output shows.
