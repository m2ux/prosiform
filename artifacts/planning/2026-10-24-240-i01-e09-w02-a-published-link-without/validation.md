## The Published Form

AC1 and AC2 are observed by the four `deferral` fixtures and the `headings` fixture, and closed over the unit suite's emission and candidate tests.

An emitted deferred link carries its text with the mark and the blanks following it removed, and the destination `rebased` produces. `[¬ the shared rules](rules.md)` publishes as `[the shared rules](rules.md)`, and `[¬Later](b%20%25%23%3F.md)` publishes as `[Later](library/b%20%25%23%3F.md)`, so the two halves of the change are visible in one line: the text moves and the destination does not.

The strip is read from the deferral operator alone, which is what the seven `dispatch` fixtures hold. A dispatched link keeps its mark and the blanks after it, because that mark is the only thing in the bundle saying the material belongs to another agent, and a dispatch's destination is the same rebased path a deferral carries. The two operators therefore part company at exactly one point, and a build that stripped by "label opens with a mark" fails all seven.

A third guard is the group of references the bundle rebased no destination for. A marked resource, a marked citation above the root, and a marked reference naming no Markdown document each publish every byte their author wrote, mark included. Only a withheld link under the root has its destination rebased, so only that one's mark is redundant. `resource/a-marked-resource-is-emitted-as-authored` and `citation/a-marked-reference-above-the-root` are unchanged by this unit and pass.

A heading carrying a deferred link is read the same way, because the heading's published text is what a renderer slugs. An ancestor title that carried a deferred link is now emitted without the mark and recorded without it, and the fragment a reference resolves against stays the slug of the authored text, so a reference into such a heading still resolves.
