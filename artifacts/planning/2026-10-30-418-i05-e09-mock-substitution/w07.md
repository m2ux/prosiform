**Component:** invocation.md

## Overview

The bundling rules, the option table and the design's link-classification clause stating what a mapping entry does.

## Problem

`invocation.md`'s option table is held to the command's own `-h` output word for word, and its bundling rules are what a caller reads to learn how references resolve. An option whose effect appears in neither leaves a caller to infer from the help text alone which references a mapping reaches, and leaves the contract silent on a behaviour the build now offers.

## Proposal

**The scene.** `make invocation` compares every `Option` row with the help output and compares required statements against the invocation guide. The design on the engineering branch states the contract the corpus encodes, and a statement a build meets and the design omits is a contract the corpus cannot hold a second build to.

**The design.**

- **An option row.** `-m, --mock` with the help text the command prints, kept identical in both places by `make invocation`.
- **A bundling rule.** A statement that a mapping entry names the identity a reference resolves to and the document the run resolves it to instead, that the substitute is the target in every respect, that a key without a fragment covers the document and every fragment of it, and that an entry is bounded by the invocation working directory rather than the writing source's root.
- **The warnings.** `W_MOCK_SUBSTITUTED` and `W_MOCK_UNUSED` stated beside the other warnings, with what each names and its exit status of `0`.
- **The design clause.** The link classification clause in `design.md` gains the mapping as a fourth thing that can decide what a reference names, so the contract states it rather than leaving it to one build's guide.
- **The version.** A new option and two new codes leave every existing fixture passing, so this is a contract minor under `docs/versioning.md`.
